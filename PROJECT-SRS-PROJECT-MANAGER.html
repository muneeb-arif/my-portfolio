1. Purpose and scope

1.1 Purpose

This specification defines the intended purpose of the portfolio and content-management product: to let a professional or studio present their work publicly on the web, while giving authenticated owners a secure place to manage all content, appearance, and operational workflows (updates, backups, and client-specific distribution where applicable).

1.2 Scope — in scope

  • A public marketing/portfolio site visible to any visitor.
  • A private dashboard for registered owners to create, edit, organize, and publish content.
  • Account lifecycle: registration, sign-in, password recovery, email verification flows as supported by the product.
  • Operational tools for power users or administrators: controlled access to backups, theme/update distribution, import/export, and diagnostic workflows.

1.3 Scope — typical exclusions (unless explicitly contracted)

  • Detailed infrastructure procurement, SLA drafting, or vendor-specific runbooks.
  • Legal/compliance sign-off (GDPR, accessibility certification); the product may support practices but does not replace counsel.
  • Custom design work beyond what the product’s themes and settings provide.

2. Product overview

The product is a web application with two faces. Visitors see a polished portfolio: projects, optional prompts or highlights, technologies, domain or niche positioning, image gallery, and ways to get in touch. Owners use the same product through a separate authenticated area (the dashboard) to control what appears on the public site, how it is organized, and how the brand looks—without needing developers for day-to-day changes.

The system is built around per-user (or per-account) content: projects, media, text blocks, navigation, and site-wide settings belong to the owner. Some capabilities are restricted to administrator roles when they affect multiple clients, system-wide updates, or sensitive operations.

In one sentence: It is an owner-operated portfolio website plus a full content, media, and operations console behind login.

3. Users and stakeholders

Role Needs (summary)
Site visitor Browse work, understand skills and focus areas, view images, contact or follow calls-to-action; fast, readable experience on mobile and desktop.
Portfolio owner Manage all public content and settings; upload images; organize projects and sections; read contact submissions; adjust what sections are visible.
Administrator (elevated) Perform sensitive or cross-cutting operations: backups, pushing theme updates to clients, import/export, and debug or sync tools—only where access is granted.
Product / delivery stakeholder Clear scope for what the app delivers to end users; predictable areas for training, support, and release communication (e.g. theme updates).

5. Functional requirements (summary)

The following numbered items are shall-level expectations at the product level. They are written for planning, acceptance alignment, and communication—not as technical tasks.

  1. FR-01 — Public access
    The system shall present a public portfolio site that does not require login, including project listing, filtering where configured, and navigation consistent with owner settings.
  2. FR-02 — Authenticated dashboard
    The system shall provide a secure dashboard available only after successful authentication, for managing content and configuration.
  3. FR-03 — Account onboarding
    The system shall support new account registration and sign-in, and shall support password reset and verification flows as implemented for the deployment.
  4. FR-04 — Project lifecycle
    Owners shall be able to create, edit, publish/unpublish (or equivalent status), and organize projects, including associated imagery and links.
  5. FR-05 — Taxonomy
    Owners shall be able to maintain categories (and related filters) used on the public project grid.
  6. FR-06 — Optional public sections
    The system shall allow owners to enable or disable major public sections (for example hero, portfolio, technologies, domains, gallery, prompts, footer) according to product rules.
  7. FR-07 — Technologies and niche content
    Owners shall be able to maintain technology/skill content and domain or niche narratives shown on the public site.
  8. FR-08 — Gallery
    Owners shall be able to manage gallery content consumed by the public gallery section.
  9. FR-09 — Custom sections and menus
    Owners shall be able to manage additional content blocks and menu structure subject to product limits.
  10. FR-10 — Contact queries
    The system shall capture visitor contact or inquiry submissions for review in the dashboard, and support appropriate status or handling as designed.
  11. FR-11 — Site settings and appearance
    Owners shall be able to change site-wide settings and visual theme options provided by the product.
  12. FR-12 — Media library
    Owners shall be able to access and manage uploaded media for use across projects and sections.
  13. FR-13 — Role-based administration
    The system shall restrict sensitive capabilities (for example backups, theme-update distribution, import/export, diagnostic tools) to users who have been granted the corresponding administrative access.
  14. FR-14 — Operational visibility
    Where theme updates or automatic updates are used, the product shall provide operators with visibility into status, logs, or dashboards appropriate to the deployment model.
  15. FR-15 — Responsive use
    Both the public site and the dashboard shall be usable on common mobile and desktop screen sizes.

6. Representative user journeys

6.1 Visitor discovers work

A visitor opens the site, scans the hero and positioning content, filters projects by category, opens a project for details, and optionally opens the gallery or uses the contact option.

6.2 Owner updates the portfolio after a new project

The owner signs in, adds or edits a project, uploads images, assigns a category, sets visibility to published, and confirms the change on the public site (same product, unauthenticated view).

6.3 Owner adjusts what the world sees

The owner turns a section off (for example prompts or gallery), reorders menus or dynamic blocks, and saves settings so the public site reflects the new structure.

6.4 Administrator rolls out a theme update

An authorized administrator uses the theme-update or client-update area to prepare or push an update, monitors logs or stats, and coordinates with clients on shared-hosting or packaged deployments where that model applies.

7. Non-functional expectations (business level)

ThemeExpectation
Security & privacy Owner data and dashboard actions are protected; only authenticated users access management functions; elevated actions require explicit admin permission.
Availability Public site and dashboard depend on hosting; stakeholders should align on hosting tier and monitoring outside this document.
Usability Primary tasks (edit project, toggle section, read inquiry) should be achievable without developer assistance for trained owners.
Maintainability (business) Content and configuration are centralized in the dashboard so marketing and portfolio updates do not require code releases for routine changes.

8. Assumptions and boundaries

  • Owners have a supported browser and internet access; the product is delivered as a web application.
  • Email delivery (verification, password reset, notifications) depends on correctly configured messaging services in the deployment environment.
  • “Administrator” capabilities are optional for single-owner deployments but exist for multi-client or commercial operator scenarios.
  • Detailed acceptance criteria for each screen belong in downstream user stories or test plans; this SRS is the umbrella description of the main application.
